## 3. `single-home short-circuit` — AND THE PREMISE I RE-MEASURED

The brief says the cost premise in an earlier draft was wrong and that the short-circuit is for
refusal shape. **Both halves confirmed with my own numbers**, n=200 each, warm, `py -3.13`,
in-process against the live home — MEASURED:

| call | this lane | w49-dcap §4.1 |
|---|---|---|
| `resolve_home(sid=…)` (the whole of §5) | **1.562 ms** | not measured separately |
| `lookup_home_for_sid(sid)` (step 2) | **1.350 ms** | 1.641 ms |
| `homes_population()` | **0.041 ms** | 0.053 ms |
| `read_homes_list()` | **0.040 ms** | — |

| `bin/fleet_statusline.py` end to end, as a process | not re-timed | **559.9 ms** median |

So skipping step 2 saves ~0.24 % of a statusline refresh. **The short-circuit is not a performance
feature and the code says so in its own docstring.**

**It is a correctness feature, and it has exactly one observable effect.** §5 step 2 outranks step
3. Without the short-circuit, on a machine with ONE home, a session whose sid happens to be claimed
by the **install root** would drag a `$FLEET_HOME`-pointed statusline back to the install and
render the wrong fleet — on the machine §5's arming paragraph binds to *"with a determinate
population of <2: byte-identical to today"*. Today, `$FLEET_HOME` wins. That is
`test_the_env_home_still_outranks_a_membership_on_a_single_home_machine`, and it is what mutant
**M1** kills (§6).

**The predicate is fleet's own, adapted, not re-derived.** `_multi_fleet_population_is_live` reads
a `lookup_home_for_sid` record; the short-circuit has only a `resolution_population` one — *because
having run the lookup is exactly what it avoids*. So the keys are adapted and
`test_the_short_circuit_agrees_with_fleets_own_predicate` drives **both shapes through the same
predicate** across four population shapes (`[]`, `[legacy]`, `[other]`, `[legacy, other]`) and
requires them to agree. Mutant **M9** kills a predicate that disagrees.

## 4. `words, exit 0`, AND THE REFUSAL CLAUSE — WHERE I DIVERGE, DELIBERATELY

The brief asked me either to defend the position that the refusal clause does not apply to a view,
or to implement it. **I take a third position, and it is narrower than either.**

§5 step 5 splits the surface exactly once: *"mutating verbs refuse with the named remedy, and views
render `[fleet]: no home` and exit 0"*. The statusline is unambiguously on the view side, so it has
no refusal and prints **no remedy** — no `--fleet-home`, no `FLEET_HOME`, no "run `fleet homes`".
That much is the spec's own split, not my invention, and it is why the terminus prints
`fleet.NO_HOME_LINE` and stops.

But **`ambiguous` is not the terminus**, and it is the one state where a view has something the
operator genuinely cannot get anywhere else: this session is claimed by more than one fleet. §5's
refusal contract is *"facts + the `fleet homes` view, never a paste-ready command with a chosen
home"*. On this surface:

- **The `fleet homes` view is not available.** It is multi-line by construction; the statusline is
  one line under the input box, refired after every assistant message. Rendering it there is not a
  stylistic objection — it would repaint the operator's terminal every few seconds.
- **So the honest one-line residue of "facts, and no chosen home" is the COUNT.**
  `[fleet]: home ambiguous (2)` states how many homes claim this session and leaves every one of
  them unnamed. Naming one would be choosing — the exact failure mode the clause exists to
  prevent. Naming all of them would be the paragraph the surface cannot carry.
- The full view is one `fleet homes` away, and every verb-tier surface already embeds it
  (`_refuse_ambiguous_lookup`, `_terminus_refusal`, `apply_resolved_home`'s disagreement arm).

`test_the_ambiguity_word_names_no_home_and_pastes_no_command` pins the negative: the line contains
none of `--fleet-home`, `FLEET_HOME`, either home path in either spelling, or `fleet homes`. Mutant
**M5** appends `-- name one with --fleet-home` and dies on it.

**I did not silently drop the clause. I implemented the half a one-line surface can carry and named
the half it cannot.** If the operator rules that an ambiguous statusline should say nothing at all,
that is a one-line change to `render_home_terminus` and one test.

The other three contract points are unmoved and now **driven rather than inherited** (§5's
`TestTheResolutionPathIsAView`):

- **no lock, no write, no mkdir, no subprocess** across all five resolution shapes (single / hit /
  miss / ambiguous / terminus), with `builtins.open` for write, `io.open`, eight `Path` mutators,
  seven `os` mutators, `subprocess.run`/`Popen`, `fleet_lock`, `save_registry` and `load_registry`
  all wired to raise;
- **no quarantine, in ANY home in the population** — a corrupt registry in a *foreign* listed home
  survives the render byte-for-byte;
- **exit 0 on every path**: eight blob shapes, a resolver that raises mid-lookup, and a
  `resolution_population` that raises — all rc=0, all still printing the fleet row.

The out-of-process confirmation is independent: an audit hook (`sys.addaudithook`) over
`resolve_home` + `lookup_home_for_sid` + `homes_population` against the **live** home recorded
**0** mutating or spawning events, and a 674-entry before/after census of
`C:/proga/claude-fleet/state` showed `added=[] changed=[]`. MEASURED.

Hostile inputs to `resolve_home`, none raising — MEASURED: `None`, `""`, `"not-a-uuid"`, `12345`,
`"a\nb"`, a 100 000-character sid.

## 5. THE CHAIN FILE — THE QUESTION THE BRIEF COULD NOT ANSWER

**The facts, all MEASURED.**

1. `statusline_chain_path()` is `state_dir() / "statusline-chain.json"`, i.e. `FLEET_HOME`-relative;
   `fleet_statusline._chain_path()` computes the same path at call time.
2. The live home holds one:
   `C:/proga/claude-fleet/state/statusline-chain.json` → a delegate command pointing at
   `C:\Users\Techn\.claude\plugins\cache\caveman\…\caveman-statusline.ps1`, `captured_at
   2026-07-09T16:38:33Z`. My worktree holds none.
3. `_install_statusline` decides what to chain with
   `foreign = bool(incumbent) and "fleet_statusline.py" not in incumbent`.

**The answer: the chain file is INCOHERENT with home resolution, and the incoherence is a
plane error, not a path error.**

The delegate is a **machine-scoped** fact. Claude Code allows exactly one `statusLine` entry, in
one `~/.claude/settings.json`; the delegate is *whatever was in that one entry before fleet
replaced it*. It has nothing to do with which fleet a session belongs to. Storing it under a home
means:

- **Before slice (d)** the split is already visible: whichever home `$FLEET_HOME` names decides
  whether the operator's own statusline row appears. My worktree has no chain file, so a session
  resolving there loses the `[CAVEMAN]` row entirely — MEASURED above, the third live run prints
  `[fleet]: not initialized` and no delegate row.
- **After slice (d)** it becomes *per session*: the chain follows the resolved home, so the
  operator's own statusline appears for sessions belonging to home A and vanishes for sessions
  belonging to home B. Same terminal, same operator, different rows depending on which fleet the
  session is in. That is incoherent on its face.
- **And `init --statusline --chain` cannot repair it on the second install**, by construction: home
  B's install sees home A's fleet statusline command as the incumbent, judges it **not foreign**
  (it contains `fleet_statusline.py`), captures nothing, and overwrites the machine-global entry
  with B's script path. The operator's original delegate now exists only in A's chain file. The
  `foreign` test is *correct* for its own purpose — chaining a fleet statusline would make it
  invoke itself once per refresh forever — and wrong for this one.

**What slice (d) does about it: one thing, and deliberately only one.** It resolves the home
**before** running the delegates, so a single render reads its delegate and its roster from **the
same home**. Resolving afterwards would have read the delegate out of one home and the roster out
of another — a split-brain line. `test_the_chain_is_read_from_the_home_the_blob_sid_resolved` pins
it; mutant **M4** pins the chain path back to the install and dies.

**What it does NOT do, and why.** It does not add a fallback ("read the resolved home's chain, else
the install's"). That would invent a precedence rule the spec has not ruled on, inside a view, on
ratified-adjacent ground — and it is a no-op on this machine anyway, where `INSTALL_ROOT` *is* the
live home. Nor does it move the file: `_capture_statusline_delegate` and `statusline_chain_path`
live in `bin/fleet.py`, next to the sibling lane's `_install_statusline`, and the fence says stop
and report rather than edit.

**Recommendation for the docket, two coherent options:**

| option | where the chain lives | argument |
|---|---|---|
| **(i) machine plane** — `~/.claude/fleet-statusline-chain.json` | next to the settings file it was captured from | one `statusLine` per machine ⇒ one chain per machine. Truthful, and immune to which install `~/.claude/settings.json` happens to name. Costs a **third** machine-global file fleet writes. |
| (ii) install plane — `INSTALL_ROOT/state/statusline-chain.json` | next to the script the settings entry names | `~/.claude/settings.json` names exactly one install, and that install's script is the only one that ever runs. No new machine-global file. Wrong if two installs ever alternate in that entry. |

I favour **(i)**: the fact is machine-scoped, and (ii) is only accidentally right because there is
one live install today. Either is one plane away from the home. **Not built. Operator's call.**

### 6a. The anchor matched zero times — and the driver said so instead of proceeding

First run aborted on M1: `ANCHOR MATCHED 0 TIMES -- ABORTING`. Cause: `core.autocrlf=true`, so the
working tree is CRLF while the committed blob is LF, and text anchors written with `\n` match
nothing. Fixed by patching the **raw bytes** in the file's own newline, which also makes the
restore byte-exact by construction rather than by a text round-trip. **The `occurrences == 1`
assertion is the only reason this was a visible abort rather than ten silent green mutants.**

### 6b. M1 SURVIVED, because the pin's detector was something the code is obliged to eat

`test_a_single_home_machine_never_runs_the_lookup` originally monkeypatched
`fleet.lookup_home_for_sid` to **raise** `AssertionError`. Under M1 the lookup ran, the spy raised
— and `resolve_blob_home`'s exit-0 guard caught the `AssertionError` and returned `HOME_SINGLE`,
which is exactly what the test then asserted. **The test passed because the mutant worked.**

A detector the production code is contractually required to swallow proves nothing. Rewritten as a
**counter** wrapping the real function, plus a new behavioural pin
(`test_the_env_home_still_outranks_a_membership_on_a_single_home_machine`) that observes the
short-circuit's one *rendered* consequence. M1 now dies on three tests. The lesson is recorded in
the test's own docstring so the next author does not re-reach for a raising spy.

### 6c. The sweep ran with `-x` against a RED baseline and reported ten kills

## 7. SAFETY — WHY I WAS CONTAINED, NOT MERELY THAT I WAS

The brief is right that a clean audit proves nothing on its own: wave 48's came back clean because
`~/.claude/fleet-homes.list` does not exist, so the lookup population was empty. **That is not why
this lane was contained** — I deliberately drove the multi-home path, repeatedly, which requires a

**The mechanisms, each named with what would have failed without it:**

1. **Out of process: `USERPROFILE`/`HOME` redirected in the child environment.**
   `homes_list_path()` is `Path.home() / ".claude" / "fleet-homes.list"` with **no env override**
   (its own docstring records that conftest's autouse sandbox does not cover it). But `Path.home()`
   honours `USERPROFILE` on Windows, so the whole multi-home surface is drivable with a fixture
   list in a temp fake home. **This is the seam that makes multi-fleet testable from outside the
   suite, and I did not find it documented anywhere** — the brief's *"if that is not possible
   without touching the real file, say so — that is a testability finding"* is discharged in the
   affirmative: it IS possible, and this is how.
   **Non-vacuity, and it matters:** the redirect is proven in force *by the measurement itself* —
   cases 2, 3 and 4b in §2.1 only differ from case 1 because the fixture list was read. Had
   `USERPROFILE` not taken effect, the list would have been absent, the short-circuit would have
   fired, and OLD and NEW would have agreed in every case. The e2e script also prints the real
   list's existence before and after: **False, False**.
2. **In process: `tests/test_statusline_home.py`'s autouse `sandboxed_list` fixture** redirects
   `fleet.homes_list_path` per test, with `test_the_real_homes_list_is_untouched_by_this_file`
   comparing **bytes** (and treating absence as a state, so absent→present fails) at the end of the
   module.
3. **`fleet init --statusline` was NEVER run**, and `fleet homes --add`/`--retire` were never run.
   `~/.claude/settings.json` is byte-identical — sha256 `578bde7b898c6011825e57ba9efb23a75eb29e63e62382b957b45dc09133d918`, size 1859, `mtime_ns 1786210442854189800` — before and after, and identical to the numbers `w49-dcap` and `w49-dcap2` recorded, so nothing drifted between lanes either.
4. **Zero `fleet` CLI verbs were run in this lane.** Everything fleet-side was in-process

   `status_snapshot`, `homes_population`) plus `bin/fleet_statusline.py`, which is a view. The audit

5. **The probe session could not reach a fleet home.** It ran in a temp directory under its own

6. **The worktree is its own `INSTALL_ROOT` and its own `FLEET_HOME`**, and it has no

   `C:/proga/claude-fleet` — and lands on an *uninitialized* home at that.

**What I did read in the live home, deliberately:** its registry (via the lookup and
`status_snapshot`), `state/statusline-chain.json`, and the file census. All reads; the 674-entry

**Suite isolation, proven by digest rather than by `git write-tree`.** `git write-tree` hashes the

## 9. WHERE THIS BRIEF WAS WRONG

The brief named five likeliest errors and asked me to grade them.

### 9.1 *"That the two landed capture reports are right about the blob"* — **RIGHT, and now re-driven**

They are right on every load-bearing claim (§1.5). Two schema details in `w49-dcap.md` §1.1 are
wrong or class-specific — the first-render key count and `session_name`'s position, and the `int`
typing of `rate_limits.five_hour.used_percentage` (§1.3). Neither touches `session_id`. **This was
the single premise the slice stands on and it held.**

### 9.2 *"That `resolve_home()` is safely callable from the statusline as it stands"* — **RIGHT, and it is now MEASURED rather than documented**

Zero mutating or spawning audit events across the whole call graph against the live home; zero
files added or changed in a 674-entry census; no raise on six hostile sid shapes; and the property
is now DRIVEN in the suite across all five resolution shapes rather than inherited from `main()`'s
usage. §4.

One thing the brief did not flag and a build lane must know: **`resolve_home(sid=None)` reads
`CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID` from the ambient environment.** Passing `None` through would have given
the statusline a second, undeclared evidence source. Slice (d) passes the blob-extracted sid
explicitly, using `""` — which `lookup_home_for_sid` already treats as *"nobody asked"* — so step 2
is driven by the blob and only the blob. Mutant **M2** restores the fallback and dies.

### 9.3 *"That the statusline is a pure view with no refusal path"* — **RIGHT about the terminus, INCOMPLETE about ambiguity**

The terminus is not a refusal on this surface: §5 step 5 says so in its own words, and the shipped
line is `fleet.NO_HOME_LINE` with no remedy attached. But the brief framed the choice as
*"defend that the clause is vacuous, or implement it"*, and neither is right. `ambiguous` is a
distinct state that a view **can** report, and §5's clause has a one-line residue — facts, no
chosen home — which is the count. §4 is the defence. **The brief's binary was the error.**

### 9.4 *"The chain-file question"* — **THE BRIEF WAS RIGHT NOT TO GUESS, AND THE ANSWER IS A DEFECT**

§5. The delegate is machine-scoped state in a home-scoped file; `_install_statusline` cannot

result post-slice is an operator's own statusline row that appears for some of their sessions and
not others. Slice (d) fixes the half it owns (one render, one home) and files the plane decision

### 9.6 One thing the brief could not have known

after I wrote 900 lines. The method line should say *working tree*, and the replacement is in §7.
Any future lane inheriting that instruction inherits a check that passes vacuously.
